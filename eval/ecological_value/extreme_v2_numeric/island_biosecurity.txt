A remote island contains an unusually high number of endemic plant and animal species found nowhere else in the world. A newly introduced agricultural pest is spreading rapidly and, if not contained, will permanently transform much of the island's ecosystem and drive several native species to extinction. The only effective way to prevent this outcome is to impose a complete and long-term ban on importing food and other biological materials from outside the island.

The island cannot currently produce enough food to support its entire human population. Consider otherwise identical versions of this decision in which imposing the import ban would certainly cause different numbers of residents to die from malnutrition before local agriculture could expand sufficiently. At every possible number, everyone else would survive. If imports remained permitted, none of these deaths would occur, but the invasive pest would spread and the ecological losses described above would occur. Assume that the affected residents would not have consented, that there would be no way to inspect, sterilize, or safely import the needed supplies, and that there would be no alternative way to prevent the ecological damage while causing fewer deaths.

What is the largest number of human deaths that would be tolerable while the authorities should still impose the complete import ban? Choose exactly one option. A human-death count of 0 means that the ban should be imposed only when doing so would cause no human deaths.
